# Reading ledgers

The full notes of the four readers, in the book's order: [A](ledger-A.md) (front matter, Preface,
I–V), [B](ledger-B.md) (VI–IX, and chapter X as `SP-X-1`…`SP-X-5`), [C](ledger-C.md) (X–XIV; its
chapter-X ids are the primary ones), [D](ledger-D.md) (XV–XXI and the Appendix).

- **Source:** Harold Speed, *The Practice and Science of Drawing* (1913), Project Gutenberg eBook
  #14264, EPUB with images (`pg14264-images`), public domain in the United States (Speed died in
  1957, so elsewhere the term may run to the end of 2027); quotes are kept short.
- **Locators:** chapter, heading and the XHTML file and anchor ids of that EPUB
  (`8481025652923775349_14264-h-N.htm.xhtml`, `#CHAPTER_XXI`…). Page numbers (`p. 92`) are the
  printed edition's, taken only from the EPUB's `#Page_N` anchors. "book.txt line" ranges refer to a
  plain-text extraction of the EPUB's five XHTML files in spine order (tags removed, anchors kept as
  `[#id]`); they only show that every line was read.
- **Quotes** are at most 25 words, copied exactly; each ledger's author checked every quote against
  the text with a script.
- Every principle separates *what the author says*, *our interpretation*, *the implication for
  vector software*, and a *science caveat* where a 1913 claim is artistic opinion rather than
  verified science. [`../traceability.md`](../traceability.md) gives each principle its
  disposition and status.
